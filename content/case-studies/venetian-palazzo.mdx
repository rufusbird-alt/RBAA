In the autumn of 2024 Rufus was first asked to advise on the future use of an apartment on the upper floors of Palazzo Barbaro, a historic palazzo on the Grand Canal. He reported to the trustees on the possible uses of the apartment. The trustees decided to sell, and invited Rufus to advise on the sale strategy.

Palazzo Barbaro has a long history beginning in the early 15th century, when the palazzo was built by the powerful Barbaro family of merchants. In 1885 the palazzo was sold to Daniel and Ariana Curtis, Bostonians seeking a more relaxed European home away from the hierarchy and strictures of Boston society at that time. Their cousin John Singer Sargent came to stay and painted in the upper-floor apartment, which was used as the painting room of Daniel and Ariana's son Ralph, who painted there alongside Sargent. Their fellow Bostonians Jack and [Isabella Stewart Gardner](https://www.gardnermuseum.org/about/isabella-stewart-gardner) came to stay often and rented the entire palazzo on several occasions. [Henry James](https://www.gardnermuseum.org/experience/collection/24715) came to stay and in 1892 was accommodated in the top-floor library. Isabella Stewart Gardner was entranced by the palazzo: it was her inspiration when she came to create her own house in Boston, Fenway Court, now the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum.

![The courtyard of Palazzo Barbaro, with its open staircase](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo00003.jpg "Photo: courtesy of Wannenes")

He made a thorough assessment of the contents, based on his visit in January 2025 referring to an old valuation from the early 2000s. Not everything was to be sold, however. Some of the works could not leave Italy, as they were categorised as works of outstanding national cultural significance. A select group of auction houses was invited to inspect the property and make recommendations.

![The library, with bookcases and a candelabrum](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo00010_web.jpg "Photo: courtesy of Wannenes")

Besides the works of national importance, one picture stood out: a striking portrait by an artist Rufus had not come across before, Le Pho. Some digging showed that this Vietnamese artist, who had worked on the fringes of the modernist circle in Paris in the 1930s, had emerged from relative obscurity and was now popular with Asian collectors. Christie's targeted that picture alone. Other auction houses made recommendations for sale in Paris, London, Florence and Genoa. After a delightful lunch at Sotheby's in London with the specialists and the trustees, Rufus and the trustees made a careful assessment of how to proceed with the sale of the contents.

![Le Pho, Portrait de dame (Portrait of a Lady)](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo-LePho.jpeg "Photo: courtesy of Christie's")

The trustees gave the green light for the Le Pho portrait to go to Christie's. Rufus contacted a former colleague, who had started at Christie's on the same day as him and is now based in New York, and he put Rufus in touch with a specialist in Paris. The picture was sent to Milan, where an export licence was obtained, and then to Paris, where a second export licence was granted, before it was shipped to Hong Kong for the 20th Century Day Sale in September 2025. It sold for HK$2,286,000 (US$293,900 / £219,300), nearly three times its low estimate. [See the lot at Christie's](https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-6549219).

For the remainder of the contents, the trustees decided on a small collection sale, to include some of the notified works of national cultural importance. That meant an Italian saleroom, and the choice was Wannenes in Genoa.

In September 2025, in close coordination with the representative of the trustees, Rufus oversaw several things during the same visit to Venice. First, representatives of the Ministry of Culture came to inspect the notified works: four pictures by Antonio Mancini and a Madonna and Child by Tintoretto of c. 1538. The required administrative changes took several more months to complete. Second, Rufus visited a possible storage facility on the mainland, a fine art shipping company with warehousing. It was to store the works retained by the family and to move the contents from the apartment to the saleroom. Third, Wannenes carried out photography in situ and some basic cataloguing. Meanwhile the apartment was on the market and available for viewing. Fourth, the loan of a painting by Ralph Curtis, second cousin of John Singer Sargent, was discussed and agreed with the Director of Ca' Rezzonico.

![Antonio Mancini, portrait of a woman, one of the notified works](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo-mancini.jpg "Photo: courtesy of Wannenes")

In March 2026 the Ministry of Culture granted the necessary licences, and at last the collection could be moved. The removal was planned for late March, but had to be abandoned because of high winds. In April and May the shipping company was committed to work for the Venice Biennale, so June was the next opportunity. Across an intensive week, the shipping company and the barge-crane operator safely removed the contents to their mainland store. The works for storage and loan stayed in store on the mainland and the selected objects for sale went on to Wannenes in Genoa.

On 29 September 2026 the Curtis collection from Palazzo Barbaro was offered at Wannenes in Genoa. It included two works of national importance, which sold successfully even though they cannot leave Italy. The 'Curtis Madonna', an early work by the 16th-century Venetian master Jacopo Robusti, better known as Tintoretto, sold for €375,150, a record for the auction house and among the ten highest prices for Tintoretto at auction worldwide. Total c. €605,000, including buyer's premium.

![Tintoretto, Madonna and Child, the 'Curtis Madonna'](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo-tintoretto.jpg "Photo: courtesy of Wannenes")

A few weeks later the painting by Ralph Curtis was delivered to Ca' Rezzonico for restoration: it is planned for display there in 2027.

![The courtyard of Palazzo Barbaro](/images/case-studies/venetian-palazzo00006.jpg "Photo: courtesy of Wannenes")

At the time of writing (October 2026) the apartment is still to be sold. The trustees' aims for the collection were met: to keep a selection of works connected with the family, to sell the surplus and to lend a work to an institution. This was a complex project that called for patience and attention to detail. Rufus was there at every step, drawing on his long-standing network of contacts in Venice and across the international art market.

*The first job is always the same: know what is there.*
