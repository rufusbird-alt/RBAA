## The Collection

The Thursby-Pelham family collection is a significant group of mainly English furniture and paintings, much of which was assembled in the first half of the 20th century. James Thursby-Pelham (1869–1947) was a celebrated collector whose pieces were used to illustrate the landmark first edition of *The Dictionary of English Furniture* by Macquoid and Edwards in 1924 — the foundational text of English furniture scholarship. His collection was published in several issues of *Country Life* and admired across the collecting community around the renowned architect, furniture scholar and advisor, R W Symonds.

I had worked with the family when at Christie’s and sold a selection of superb furniture and lesser pictures in two sales in November and December 2007. The pieces I was asked to assess in 2026 were the final part of that larger collection, which had remained in the same family for some 80 years. When the time came to bring the collection to market, the challenge was clear: how to position a large, multi-period holding in a way that reflected both its historical significance and current market values, without fragmenting it through selective cherry-picking.

## The Advisory Strategy

Rather than extract the highest-value pieces for separate sale I recommended an integrated dispersal strategy: to research and authenticate overlooked pieces within the collection, use historic findings to build narrative appeal, and market the group — with one exception — as a coherent whole. The exception was a charcoal portrait drawing by John Singer Sargent, which was consigned to Christie’s. The drawing was significantly different from the rest of the group, and by a star named artist, so I advised a different strategy for this one piece. I negotiated favourable terms; it was entered into Christie’s ‘Day Sale’ on 30 June 2026 and sold for £82,550 — more than doubling its low auction estimate.

## Identifying Hidden Value

Within the larger group, I identified works that warranted deeper research. One painting — a small portrait on panel, clearly a 16th-century picture by a Flemish artist — was singled out for careful analysis. The auction house I worked with — Sworders — sought the views of Dr Alexandra Zvereva, scholar of Gillis Claeissens (1526–1605), a Bruges-based artist, re-identified as a named artist in 2017. I directed Sworders to undertake research into the work’s attribution and provenance. Their research confirmed the Claeissens attribution; separately research into the clues left by the Christie’s stencilled number on the reverse also divulged that Henry Huth was the owner before Thursby-Pelham.

This single attribution discovery had a strategic effect: it demonstrated the collection’s scholarly depth and the quality of connoisseurship required to assemble it. It also signalled to the market that careful research would accompany the entire sale.

## Execution and Results

Sworders conducted the dispersal across a series of sales, with the research-authenticated pieces kept together as a group, rather than isolated. The Claeissens portrait sold for £53,300 — a result that reflected both the artist’s emerging market recognition and the confidence generated by scholarship. The overall collection realised significantly higher totals than would have been achieved through fragmentary sales. By positioning the collection as a coherent group — complete with scholarly validation and documented provenance — each piece benefited from the strength of the whole.

## Why This Matters for Collectors

This transaction illustrates a principle central to collection management: sometimes the greatest value lies not in simply focusing on the individual stars, but in recognising and enhancing the coherence of the wider group itself. Families holding large collections often face the temptation to isolate and sell the “best” pieces first. This approach leaves residual material undervalued and opportunity cost.

A better strategy — one that requires genuine expertise to execute — is to understand the collection as a historical and market entity.

**For advisors and collectors alike, the Thursby-Pelham sales demonstrate that scholarship, judgement and strategy are inseparable from market success.**
