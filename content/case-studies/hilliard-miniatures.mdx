Some years ago Rufus was invited to visit a country house, not far from Reading. Here was a historic collection, in fact two merged collections, which had been moved from house to house, from generation to generation. The family sought guidance on the value of the contents and what to do. The house would be sold; some of the contents too, and some retained.

While making a detailed assessment of the furniture and pictures, in the basement he came across a velvet-covered panel hung with miniatures, two of which looked possibly Elizabethan: certainly interesting and worthy of further examination.

![The basement where the miniature was discovered](/images/case-studies/hilliard-miniatures-basement.jpg)

![The basement, detail](/images/case-studies/hilliard-miniatures-basement2.jpg)

The principal contents were sold successfully at a well-known regional saleroom. But the miniatures were held back for research. Rufus showed them to Christie's miniatures specialist, a former colleague, who gave an informal auction value and opinion, suggesting strongly that they might be by Nicholas Hilliard. Discussing this with the owners, Rufus recommended a pause and further research.

![Hilliard miniature, obverse](/images/case-studies/hilliard-1-large.jpg)

He undertook research on the sitters at the Heinz Archive, part of the National Portrait Gallery. The client joined him on a subsequent visit and made a suggestion: could the sitter with the long blond hair in fact be a man? The client thought the sitter in one of the two miniatures could perhaps even depict Shakespeare's patron, Henry Wriothesley, 3rd Earl of Southampton. The sitter did indeed bear a resemblance to an accepted depiction of Wriothesley in The Fitzwilliam Museum, Cambridge.

![Hilliard miniature, detail](/images/case-studies/hilliard-2-large.jpg)

At this point, Rufus introduced the clients to the Tudor historian and Hilliard specialist Dr Elizabeth Goldring. Dr Goldring agreed that the portrait was likely to depict the 3rd Earl of Southampton. In concert with The Limner Company, a private sale was concluded.
