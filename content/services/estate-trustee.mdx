A great house, an inherited collection, a palazzo in <SmallCaps>Venice</SmallCaps> or a manoir in <SmallCaps>Normandy</SmallCaps>. The trustees — often new to the assets, sometimes newly appointed — are asked to make decisions about art, furniture, silver and archives that they have rarely had time to examine. Beneficiaries are waiting. Insurers are waiting. The tax authorities are waiting.

What is needed first is not a sale strategy. It is simply to know what is there.

Rufus begins by walking the building. Room by room. He catalogues what is present and reconstructs the provenance of what has moved. He tests attributions that have been inherited with the property, many of which have not been looked at in a generation. He values for probate, for insurance, for family distribution, and for eventual sale — separately, because each asks a different question. He coordinates storage, conservation, property liaison, and the quiet, discreet sales that are sometimes the right answer.

He has done this for trustees of a palazzo in <SmallCaps>Venice</SmallCaps>, and for a number of other estates and private clients. In every case, the work is undertaken at an agreed fee, with full disclosure, and with a willingness to be there for as long as the estate continues to need counsel.

*The first job is to know what is there. Every decision improves after that.*
